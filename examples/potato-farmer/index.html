The man behind the mud

Earl Thompson, 63, has loved potatoes since he was six and stole a raw Russet from his grandpa's cellar. He ate it like an apple. He has no regrets and tells this story to everyone.

Every morning at 4:40 AM, Earl walks his 40 acres, talks to the rows ("grow big, my round friends"), and names one potato per harvest. This year's chosen one is called Kevin. Kevin will not be eaten. Kevin has his own shelf.

"People ask why I never left Idaho," Earl says. "Have you ever held a perfect Yukon Gold at sunrise? Didn't think so."

"My wife left me for a corn farmer in '09. The potatoes stayed. Potatoes don't leave." — Earl
